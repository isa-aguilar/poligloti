# Native expressions seed: English

Idioms and colloquialisms in real use. One per line: `**expression** | meaning | register | e.g. "example"`

## Work

- **to be on the same page** | to be aligned, to agree | neutral/business | e.g. "Let's make sure we're on the same page before the call."
- **to touch base** | to get in contact briefly | neutral/business | e.g. "I'll touch base with you next week."
- **to get the ball rolling** | to get something started | neutral | e.g. "Let's get the ball rolling on the project."
- **to think outside the box** | to think creatively, unconventionally | neutral | e.g. "We need to think outside the box here."
- **to cut corners** | to do things badly or cheaply to save time or money | neutral | e.g. "We can't cut corners on safety."
- **the elephant in the room** | the obvious problem nobody mentions | neutral | e.g. "Let's address the elephant in the room."
- **to be snowed under** | to have far too much work | colloquial | e.g. "I'm snowed under this week."

## Everyday and social

- **to break the ice** | to ease the initial tension | neutral | e.g. "He told a joke to break the ice."
- **a piece of cake** | something very easy | colloquial | e.g. "Don't worry, the exam was a piece of cake."
- **to cost an arm and a leg** | to be very expensive | colloquial | e.g. "That car cost an arm and a leg."
- **to hit the nail on the head** | to be exactly right | neutral | e.g. "You hit the nail on the head with that comment."
- **to be under the weather** | to feel slightly ill | colloquial | e.g. "I'm feeling a bit under the weather today."
- **to spill the beans** | to reveal a secret | colloquial | e.g. "Come on, spill the beans!"
- **once in a blue moon** | very rarely | colloquial | e.g. "We only go out once in a blue moon."
- **to call it a day** | to stop working for the day | colloquial | e.g. "It's late, let's call it a day."
- **to get cold feet** | to back out because of fear or nerves | colloquial | e.g. "He got cold feet before the presentation."
- **the best of both worlds** | the advantages of two things at once | neutral | e.g. "Working from home is the best of both worlds."
